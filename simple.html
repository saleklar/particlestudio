Hold Ctrl while dragging the emitter to record its motion. Captured points: 0.
Each emitter has its own Particle Image, Emitter, Motion, Attractor, Deflector, Appearance, Randomness and Mesh Deformer settings plus its own position — the sidebar edits the highlighted one. The 🌐 checkbox on a panel makes it global: that emitter’s values drive all emitters (only one owner per panel). Bodies, tint, curves, playback and export are always global. Click a name to edit it, ✎ or double-click to rename, 👁 toggles it, ⧉ duplicates it. In the Spine export each emitter becomes a parent bone (using its name) with its particles as child bones.

5

A square warp grid — drag its vertices to sculpt a flow. Particles crossing the grid bend along the deformation, as if flying around a 3D object behind them. Weight = how strictly they follow (1 = locked to the flow, 0.1 = gentle nudge). Baked into Spine + PNG exports.

Paths

paths rotate with the 3D camera
Global path library — every type becomes an editable spline. Click a path in the list to select it, then sculpt on canvas: drag the green anchors, drag the line to move the whole path, double-click the line to add a vertex, double-click an anchor to remove it. Use a path in the Emitter panel (“Follow path”: the emitter rides it, Path cycles = laps per animation loop) and/or in Motion (“Path follow”: particles steer along it, Path weight = how strictly).

Camera keyframes: scrub the sliders to a pose, press “+ Key at Playhead” — the key is stamped at the current loop position (%). Simulates particles in 3D: velocities spread into depth, the Emitter’s Depth slider gives the shape thickness, and the camera orbits the emitter (single emitter) or the canvas centre. Add lights and drag them on the canvas; the sliders edit the selected light. With Shadows on, particles outside every light’s range fade to black (Feather = how soft the edge is); with Shadows off, lights only add their colour. Light colours multiply into the existing particle tint — and into the slot tint of the Spine export, so the lighting survives in Spine. Point = radius falloff · Spot = cone (Angle + Cone) · Directional = even fill light.
